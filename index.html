01 — PROBLEM

The Engineering Problem

Imagine a modern smart factory where hundreds of sensors and digital devices work together. Sensors detect temperature, position and machine status, while controllers make decisions in real time.

Engineering Challenges

Electrical noise from motors and power cables corrupts digital signals

Temperature variation across the factory floor affects device operation

Multiple interconnected digital devices must share signals reliably

Digital signals must be fast and reliable for real-time control

Continuous operation requires low power consumption and thermal stability

The Engineering Question

Which logic family should we use — TTL or CMOS?

02 — REQUIREMENTS

System Requirements

Before selecting a logic family, we must identify what the factory's digital system actually needs.

🛡️

Reliability

Reliable operation in an industrial environment with vibration, dust, and continuous duty cycles.

⚡

Noise Immunity

Ability to tolerate unwanted electrical disturbances generated by motors, cables, and switching loads.

🔀

Fan-Out

Ability of one digital output to drive multiple input loads without degrading the logic signal.

⚡

Speed

How quickly a logic circuit responds to an input change — critical for real-time control decisions.

🔋

Low Power

Reducing unnecessary static power consumption to lower heat generation and operating costs.

🌡️

Temperature Tolerance

Ability to operate reliably across the full temperature range present in an industrial environment.

03 — PARAMETERS

Understanding Logic Parameters

Before comparing TTL and CMOS, we need to understand the key electrical parameters that characterise any logic family.

Fan-In

Fan-in represents the number of inputs a logic gate can accept simultaneously without incorrect operation.

Fan-Out

Fan-out represents the number of logic gate inputs that one output can drive reliably without voltage degradation.

Propagation Delay

Propagation delay (tpd) is the time taken for a logic output to respond to a change at its input.

Noise Margin

Noise margin is the voltage difference between the guaranteed output level and the required input threshold — it represents the system's tolerance to noise.

Power Dissipation

Power dissipation in CMOS is near-zero when static and only occurs during switching, while TTL has a constant static supply current path through its BJT output structure.

08 — TRADE-OFFS

Trade-Offs

Every technology choice involves trade-offs. Understanding CMOS advantages and engineering considerations leads to a better design.

✓ CMOS Advantages

✓
Low Static Power Consumption CMOS draws negligible current when not switching — ideal for continuously powered industrial systems.
✓
High Input Impedance MOSFET gates draw near-zero input current, enabling very high fan-out capability.
✓
Good Noise Margins CMOS output swings rail-to-rail, providing larger noise margins than TTL — important in an electrically noisy factory.
✓
Wide Operating Voltage Range 74HC can operate over a wide VCC range, providing design flexibility across different system voltages.

⚠ Engineering Considerations

⚠
Avoid Floating CMOS Inputs Unconnected CMOS inputs can assume random logic states. Always tie unused inputs to VCC or GND.
⚠
Control Capacitive Loading High capacitive loads increase propagation delay and dynamic power. Minimise trace lengths and load capacitance.
⚠
Use Good PCB Layout Practice Proper routing, layer stack-up and signal integrity practices are critical for reliable high-speed CMOS operation.
⚠
Proper Grounding and Decoupling Place decoupling capacitors close to each IC supply pin. Use a solid ground plane to reduce noise coupling.
⚠
Fast Edges Can Produce Ringing High-speed CMOS transitions can cause transmission-line reflections on long PCB traces. Use series termination where required.

Conclusion

The smart factory digital subsystem is operating smoothly. Signals travel cleanly between sensors, the controller, and actuators. The right logic family makes all the difference.
